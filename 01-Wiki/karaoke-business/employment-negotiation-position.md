# Employment Negotiation Position

**Summary:** Notes working out a negotiating position with the employer: an estimate of what a back wages and unpaid overtime claim would be worth, and what would be accepted instead of filing it — a one-year contract with actual control over marketing, starting with the Google Ads password. It also includes how to frame the deal so the manager is not blamed for agreeing to it.

## Key Points
- Estimated claim value if filed: **$15,000–20,000** for back wages, unpaid overtime and everything combined.
- What is wanted instead: **hired for a year**, with control over the things being held responsible for.
- Specifically named: **the Google Ads password** — no more responsibility for results without access.
- Framing offered: the agreement is a **net financial benefit**, so it should be treated as an expense, not a concession.
- Explicit instruction not to lead with the lawsuit when relaying it — state the ask, not the threat.

## The Position
> Reasons you should agree to this: he will lose roughly $15,000, maybe $20,000, if I sue for back wages, unpaid overtime and everything combined. Essentially I'm trying to increase sales — so if you agree to this, I will even find a way where your boss can't blame you for accepting it, because what you did was beneficial overall financially. It should just be considered an expense.

> If they agree to this, I'll sign a contract saying so — as long as I'm hired for a year and in control of what I want to be in control of. That means the Google Ads password. I don't want you in control of anything.

> Don't tell him that. Just tell him: this is what I want. That's the price.

## What This Is Really About
The whole argument running through these notes is being held responsible for sales while having no control over the things that produce sales. Both [[letter-to-manager-store-performance|the letter]] and [[manager-argument-the-numbers|the numbers]] repeat the same line — *"I had no control of the ads, and I still don't."*

So the ask is narrow and coherent: give the responsibility and the controls to the same person. A one-year term makes it testable — a year is long enough for marketing changes to show up in sales figures and short enough that nobody is committing forever.

## Worth Getting Right Before Acting
- **Get the wage claim checked by an employment attorney before quoting a number.** $15,000–20,000 is an estimate, and New York wage law has specific rules on overtime, spread-of-hours pay, and record-keeping penalties. The real figure could be higher or lower, and quoting a wrong number in a negotiation is worse than quoting none.
- **A lawsuit and a one-year contract are hard to hold together.** Signing an employment agreement usually involves releasing existing claims. If the back wages are real and worth pursuing, that is a separate decision from the job — not a bargaining chip to be spent.
- **Put the control terms in writing.** "Control of Google Ads" means account ownership, the password, and no changes without sign-off. Verbal agreement on this specific point is worth nothing, since the entire dispute is about who is accountable for which decisions.
