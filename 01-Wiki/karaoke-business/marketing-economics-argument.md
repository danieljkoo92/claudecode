# Marketing Economics Argument

**Summary:** The sharpest financial section of the dispute — it prices the specific opportunities being passed up. Cutting cost-per-click from 30¢ to 20¢ saves $500 a month. A $1,000 SEO rebuild pays for itself in two months. And roughly 80% of current customers already know the venue exists, which means the marketing is being aimed at the wrong people entirely.

## Key Points
- Ads currently run at about **30¢ per click**. At 20¢, a **$1,500/month spend becomes $1,000** — **$500 saved every month**.
- Paying **$1,000 for a proper SEO website** pays back in **two months** at that rate, then earns.
- **~80% of customers already know the store exists.** Marketing to them is the slow way.
- **Changing the website is free. Changing the marketing is free.** Only the saving mentality costs.
- The BYOB post that "worked" **worked by luck** — post enough and one will spike.

## The Cost-Per-Click Argument
> Right now the ad cost is about 30 cents per click. If you get it down to 20 cents, the $1,500 a month you're spending becomes $1,000 — that's $500 saved. Does that calculation not happen in your head?
>
> Instead of trying to save the store's money, using your head to raise sales is ten or twenty times more effective.

## The SEO Payback
> If you hire someone to build the website properly for SEO and it costs $1,000 — that's making money. In two or three months you're earning that $500 a month back.
>
> But you only calculate the money going out. You don't calculate what comes back from it, so you don't see the upside at all. You only see money leaving, so you try not to spend any.

## The 80% Problem
This is the most important strategic observation in the notes:

> About 80% of the customers who come already know the store exists. So you have to market to the people who **don't** know — that's where the effect is fast.
>
> The people who do know and are booking are all doing it for birthdays and events. Every reservation is an event or a birthday. Who else is spending $250 an hour on a room? Who pays $250 for bread ten years past its expiry date?
>
> No matter how well you treat these people, without an incentive they won't come more often. The most effective thing available right now is getting someone to recommend us to a friend for that friend's birthday.

That last line is the whole retention strategy in one sentence — and it explains why the [[birthday-packages-and-themed-rooms|birthday packages]] and the referral-driven comping matter more than anything else on the list.

## On Testing Ads Properly
> When you run an ad, track how much money came in from it and what's left over. And keep changing the ad style to find what works best.
>
> The BYOB post we're running now — we posted four or five, this one performed best, so we keep posting it. That's not because the photo is good or the format works. **It got lucky.** Post any videos or pictures and the more you post, the more likely one gets the most views. Something spiking once doesn't mean it keeps spiking. Other influencers post the identical video and the view counts differ enormously.

## On Using AI for the Numbers
> You do everything with a calculator, which is nuts. You know Excel but only the easy parts, not formulas. I don't know Excel either — so I use AI to do the calculations and then use another AI to check whether it's correct.
>
> Take a picture of the bills, a picture of the expenses, give it the numbers and tell it this is expenses, this is profit, this is sales. It can work all of it out.
>
> But on instant-answer mode you get about **60% correct** on irregular math, because it reads in chunks rather than every number. Set up properly it works like a junior accountant — **95–99% correct**. Have a second AI check the first and it's near enough exact.

That is a genuinely useful distinction: the tool is only as good as the setup, and checking one model's arithmetic with another is a real technique.

## What This Costs to Fix
Nothing. That is the point being made repeatedly:
> The equipment is trash, the sound is trash, we don't give drink service, the price is expensive and keeps going up. All of that has problems and all of it is being saved on. **But changing the marketing is free.**
