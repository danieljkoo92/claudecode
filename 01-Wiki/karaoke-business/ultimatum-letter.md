# Ultimatum Letter

**Summary:** The most measured version of the whole dispute — an opening statement framed as one last conversation about sales rather than an attack. It concedes what the manager is genuinely good at, states plainly where the disagreement is, and then makes the case with a single number: what $100 of giveaway alcohol actually buys.

## Key Points
- Framed as **"don't take this like a threat"** — and states outright that he wants to work there.
- Concedes the manager is **very good at saving money**, and that he himself is not.
- The claim: **marketing can be done cheaply and organically**, at very little alcohol cost.
- **$100 of giveaway budget = 470 shots, 1,400 shooters, 140 mixed drinks, or 100 of the most expensive cocktails.**
- The giveaway is **selective** — targeted at people who live nearby and have a high chance of returning.

## The Opening
> Okay, so don't take this like a threat, and I really don't want to do this, but I am giving you an ultimatum right now.
>
> First, I want to say I want to work here, and I think the potential for sales is way more than you could even think. I don't want to have to make this an ultimatum, but you leave me no choice — because I do want to work here and I do want to increase sales, and you're stopping me.
>
> Don't get mad about what I'm saying. Let's have this one last conversation about sales and my future here. Your future — not at this company, but at this location — might depend on it as well.

## The Concession
> I believe you and me will work great together, judging by the fact that you are very good at saving money, and that's what I am not that great at.
>
> But I believe I can market and fill this store with people way better than you can, and I can show you right now the proof of why you're ruining it — because you're cheap. I understand that every dollar you save is an extra dollar of profit. I totally understand that.
>
> What I don't think you understand is that marketing can be done extremely cheaply, organically, with very little cost to us in the cost of alcohol.

## The $100 Question
> Do you know how much I can increase sales with a $100 budget to give away in alcohol?
>
> With a $100 budget for service, given selectively to people who live around here and have a high chance of returning:
>
> **470 shots. 1,400 shooters. 140 mixed drinks. Or 100 of the most expensive cocktails.**

## Why This Is the Version to Actually Send
Everything else in this dispute — [[letter-to-manager-store-performance|the letter]], [[manager-argument-the-numbers|the numbers]] — makes the same case but buries it in anger. This version does three things the others don't:

1. **Opens with wanting to stay.** That reframes everything after it as an attempt to fix the business rather than a resignation letter.
2. **Gives the other person something real.** "You're very good at saving money and I'm not" is true and disarming, and it sets up the whole argument as complementary skills rather than a contest.
3. **Reduces the argument to one number.** $100 buys 470 shots. Nobody can argue with that, and it makes the scale of the giveaway concrete — the fear is that comping is expensive, and this shows the actual size of "expensive."

The word "ultimatum" is the one thing working against it. The content is a proposal; calling it an ultimatum invites the reaction that the rest of the letter is trying to avoid.
