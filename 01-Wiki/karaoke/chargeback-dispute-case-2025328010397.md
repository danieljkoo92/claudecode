## The Argument Made
The assistant manager was present on 11/08/2025 and received no complaints about payment. The group arrived at 9:35 PM on 11/07 and checked out at 12:39 AM on 11/08, staying 3 hours and 2 minutes, during which they did BYOB.

At checkout the itemized receipt was provided before charging. **$40 for BYOB** was the only item added to the bill before tax and gratuity, besides the room rate. The customers paid **$360 in cash**, resulting in a final card charge of **$130.78**. ID was verified against the credit card and no concerns were raised.

## Why This One Is Worth Understanding
This is the case that most clearly shows why split cash-and-card payments get disputed. The customer sees a total, hands over $360 cash, and then a separate $130.78 hits the card — and a month later that second number looks unfamiliar on a statement.

**The fix:** write the cash amount and the remaining card amount on the itemized receipt, and say both numbers out loud when handing it over. It costs nothing and removes the entire basis for this type of dispute.

## The BYOB Fee in Context
$40 flat is what is being charged now. The [[marketing-and-byob-strategy|BYOB strategy note]] proposes moving to per-person pricing instead — $3 per person, $20 unlimited beer, $10 wine and alcohol — which for a group this size would have produced considerably more than $40.
