# NDA and IP Assignment Agreement (Template)

**Summary:** A full-length confidentiality, non-disclosure, non-use, non-circumvention and intellectual property assignment agreement, written to protect apps, websites, software, hospitality systems and AI workflows. It is a blank template with signature blocks and three exhibits. It is unusually broad — it covers information disclosed before signing, bans putting anything into AI tools, and assigns ownership of anything the receiving party builds or even suggests.

## Key Points
- Covers **36 sections** plus Exhibits A, B and C.
- Applies to information disclosed **before, on, or after** the signing date.
- Explicitly bans pasting anything into **ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, GitHub, Figma, Notion, Supabase** and similar tools without written consent.
- All **Work Product** is owned by the Owner from the moment of creation — including suggestions and improvements.
- No portfolio, resume, LinkedIn or case study use for **ten years**.
- Non-circumvention runs **three years** from last disclosure.

## What It Protects
The definition of Confidential Information is deliberately wide, covering:

- **Products:** apps, websites, SaaS platforms, dashboards, admin panels, ordering systems, hospitality and bar and karaoke systems, reservation systems, menu systems, voice systems, SOP systems, marketing and automation systems
- **Technical:** source code, databases, schemas, APIs, API keys, **prompts and system prompts**, AI-agent instructions, automation flows, architecture, deployment notes
- **Design:** wireframes, Figma and Canva files, layouts, logos, copy, ads, landing pages, user flows
- **Business:** monetization plans, pricing, SEO and ad strategy, customer and vendor lists, financial projections, pitch materials
- **Naming:** business names, app names, domains, slogans, trademarks, social handles, roadmaps
- Anything the receiving party creates that **reflects or is derived from** the above

Information does not need to be marked "confidential" to be protected.

## The Unusual Clauses
These are the parts that go further than a standard NDA:

**Section 3 — Prior disclosures.** Anything already shared before signing is still covered. No prior disclosure counts as public or waived just because it happened first.

**Section 8 — No copying.** No screenshots, screen recordings, downloads, exports, prints or backups without written permission. Any unauthorized copy becomes the Owner's property.

**Section 9 — No reconstruction.** Memory-based, note-based and screenshot-based reconstruction are all prohibited. If the receiving party later builds something substantially similar, that similarity **may itself be treated as evidence** it was derived.

**Section 10 — No AI tool exposure.** Nothing may be entered into any AI, coding, design, cloud or file-sharing tool. Named: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, Perplexity, Codex, Cursor, Replit, Lovable, Bolt, V0, GitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket, Vercel, Netlify, Supabase, Firebase, Figma, Canva, Wix, Google Drive, Dropbox, Notion, Slack, Discord, Trello, Asana, Jira.

**Section 18 — Suggestions.** Any idea, fix, feature request or improvement the receiving party suggests is Work Product and belongs to the Owner. No royalties, equity or credit claimed from it.

**Section 20 — Names and domains.** Registering a similar domain, handle or brand name means transferring it to the Owner at no cost.

**Section 21 — Account access.** A 19-point list: no sharing credentials, no changing billing or recovery emails, no exporting data, no hidden backups, no duplicate repositories, no locking the Owner out.

## The Fairness Carveouts
Two clauses cut the other way, which is what makes the agreement defensible:

- **Section 7** does not stop the receiving party using general skills and knowledge acquired lawfully without reference to the confidential material.
- **Section 16** does not require assignment of anything proven through **clear written records** to have been developed entirely independently.

That second one matters practically: anyone signing this should document their pre-existing work in **Exhibit B before starting**, or it is presumed to be Work Product.

## Structure
| Sections | Cover |
|---|---|
| 1–5 | Purpose, consideration, prior disclosures, definitions, trade secrets |
| 6–12 | Confidentiality, non-use, no copying, no reverse engineering, no AI tools, no delegation, no portfolio use |
| 13–19 | Ownership, Work Product, IP assignment, independent inventions, pre-existing materials, suggestions |
| 20–22 | Domains and names, account access, non-circumvention |
| 23–30 | Exclusions, legal disclosure, return and deletion, waiver, remedies, fees, indemnification |
| 31–36 | Term and survival, governing law, severability, entire agreement, e-signatures, acknowledgment |
| Exhibits | A, B (pre-existing materials), C (authorized purpose) |

## Before Using It
- Fill in the Owner and Receiving Party blocks, the effective date, and the governing law and venue section.
- **Exhibit C defines the authorized purpose** — the non-use clause points at it, so leaving it blank weakens the whole agreement.
- Have a lawyer look at it before anyone signs. Sections 9 and 18 in particular are aggressive enough that a court might narrow them, and an agreement that gets partially struck down is worse than one drafted to hold.
