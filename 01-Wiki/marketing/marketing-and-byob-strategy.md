# Marketing and BYOB Strategy

**Summary:** A set of strategy notes arguing for a change in direction at the karaoke venue: stop chasing alcohol sales, focus on filling rooms, then charge for BYOB — which produces similar revenue at zero cost. It also covers a marketing shift toward Long Island because the local customer base is tapped out, fixes needed on the website and Google ads, and a Korean "boss friend package" pricing label.

## Key Points
- **Give up on alcohol sales, focus on filling the rooms.** Once full, charge for BYOB — same revenue as alcohol sales, **$0 cost**.
- Proposed BYOB pricing: **$3 per person**, and/or **$20 unlimited beer**, **$10 wine and alcohol**.
- If it gets too busy to track, a **bouncer checks bags**; push packages on anyone not doing BYOB.
- **70–80% of people who see the Google ads are women** — the ads have to look nice and be phrased properly.
- Marketing focus should move toward **Long Island** — coupons were rejected locally because the store is old.
- Website fixes outstanding: hours wrong for Fri–Sun, room QR codes still point to the old site.

## The BYOB Argument
The reasoning: alcohol sales come with cost of goods, and the venue is not winning on them. Filling rooms is the actual constraint. Once the rooms are full, charging for BYOB brings in the same money as current alcohol sales — or more — with no cost to the business at all.

Pricing floated:
| Option | Price |
|---|---|
| Per person BYOB fee | $3 |
| Unlimited beer | $20 |
| Wine and alcohol | $10 |

Enforcement: if it gets busy enough that staff cannot catch people bringing bottles in, put a bouncer on bag checks. Anyone who does not BYOB gets pushed toward packages instead.

## Why Change the Customer Base
People were rejecting coupons because the store is old. If the percentage of people who do karaoke at all is as low as discussed, then simply being here this long has probably used up most of the customers who have better options nearby. That leaves two moves: **push BYOB all the way and start charging for it**, or **market to Long Island** for a fresh customer base.

Two observations recorded alongside this:
- "When's the last time you saw other Korean people?" — the Korean customer base has thinned.
- "Korean people keep asking for food." — unmet demand for food service.

## Comping Drinks — The Math
A note written in Korean pushes back on treating a comped shot as lost money:

> 월래 올려고 하는사람 샷 하나 더 주는거 느소냐인거 맞아요. 근데 이거 10번 중에 1 번 만 누가 1면 - 2 명 더 대리고 오면 70.33 새일즈 올리는거에요

Roughly (the original has typos): *yes, giving an extra shot to someone who was already coming is a loss. But if even 1 time out of 10 that person brings 1–2 more people with them, it raises sales by 70.33.* The point being that comps should be judged on whether they bring extra heads through the door, not on the cost of the drink.

## Packages
Make Korean packages. Call them the **"boss friend package"** — tell anyone who asks that it is just a label to remind employees what discounted rate to charge the boss's friends.
