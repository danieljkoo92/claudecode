One — Canon So Far
I will keep this as the factual base and not add motives or details you did not give.
Core Role
• One is the major main character within the five-person ensemble.
• He becomes the leader naturally. Nobody appoints him.
• He simply says what he thinks should happen or what he wants to do.
• His plans are usually better than everyone else’s, so people follow him.
• Leadership becomes necessary because the other kids do not function or move efficiently without him directing things.
• He treats leadership like a full-time responsibility and sees the group as family.
• He becomes somewhat controlling, but not excessively.

Family
• One grew up poor.
• He has no information about his father.
• His mother has been sick for as long as he can remember.
• She is unable to work.
• One knows she is sick.
• He sometimes picks up her medicine and runs errands for her.
• His mother is loving.
• He has one younger sister.
• One and his sister are very close.
• As he becomes involved in crime, he distances himself from her somewhat to keep her away from that world.
• He remains present whenever she needs him.
Personality
• Quiet.
• Intimidating.
• Charismatic without trying.
• Careful and strategic with words.
• Good at reading weaknesses.
• Good at seeing dynamics, causes, and perspectives other people miss.
• Does not chase leadership or attention.
• Fights when he believes it is needed.
Natural Ability
• One can watch something happen, mentally reimagine it, analyze why and how it worked, and copy or improve it.
• He sees multiple perspectives at once.
• This applies to fighting, people, situations, and eventually business/criminal organization.
• At first, he does not fully use this ability outside physical conflict.
Early Flaw
• One solves problems with violence because violence has always worked for him.
• He has not needed to use his intelligence as much as he should.
• Later, a situation forces him to think rather than fight.
• That becomes the point where he realizes his mind is more valuable than simply beating people up.
Entry Into Crime
• One sees older kids selling drugs in an unorganized, careless way.
• He recognizes that they are operating inefficiently.
• He finds a better way to organize and position what they are doing.
• This lets him take customers from others and begin making real money.
• Later, he realizes money can materially change his life and help with real problems.
Early Major Mistake
• Before age 13, One hurts the wrong person.
• That is the first clear proof that solving everything with violence creates problems he cannot punch his way out of.
