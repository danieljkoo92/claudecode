# Welfare Spending Timeline — HTML Project

**Summary:** A built HTML page titled "U.S. Social Program Spending + Black Single Mothers" — an interactive timeline placing federal social program spending alongside demographic statistics from the 1960s through the 2020s. Roughly 900 lines of finished, styled HTML sit in the notes file. This records what the project is; the code itself stays in the archive.

## Key Points
- Title: **"U.S. Social Program Spending + Black Single Mothers"**.
- Format: a vertical timeline grouped by year, with colour-coded category badges.
- Categories tagged: **Welfare, Housing, Food, Family**.
- Each entry pairs a description with a spending or population figure.
- Complete, styled HTML — not a draft.

## Structure
Each year group contains one or more events, and each event carries:
- A **category badge** (Welfare / Housing / Food / Family)
- A **description** of the policy change or demographic shift
- A **figure line** — dollar spend or population share

## Years Covered
The timeline runs from the 1960s through the 2020s, with detailed entries at 1974–1975 (Section 8 vouchers, Title IV-D child support enforcement), 1980 (AFDC peak in real value, Reagan-era cuts beginning 1981), 1990–1991 (recession expanding food stamp rolls), and 1996 (PRWORA replacing AFDC with TANF as a capped block grant).

## Before Publishing This Anywhere
Every figure on the page is presented as fact, so each one needs a source attached before it goes public. Specifically worth checking:
- The AFDC, food stamp, Section 8 and TANF spending figures — real-dollar vs nominal makes a large difference to the story the chart tells, and the entries mix both.
- The demographic percentages, which should cite the specific Census or CDC series.
- The causal language. Several entries describe a policy and a demographic shift in the same breath; correlation in a timeline reads as causation to anyone looking at it, and that is a claim that needs evidence rather than adjacency.
