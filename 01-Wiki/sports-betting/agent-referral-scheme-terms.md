# Agent Referral Scheme — Internal Terms

**Summary:** The internal economics of a sports betting agent arrangement, headed with an explicit instruction not to send it to potential clients. It sets out what the referrer earns from player losses, the bonus structure offered to players, and the split on bonus money.

## Key Points
- Marked **"DON'T SEND THIS TO POTENTIAL CLIENTS"** — these are the internal terms.
- Referrers take **10% of what players lose**, and lose nothing when players win.
- Paid **in cash every Monday**.
- Players get a **40% bonus** on first deposit, **20% on every deposit** after.
- Bonus money is not real cash — the referrers take 10% each of it to gamble with.
- Odds match standard betting websites.

## The Terms as Written
1. You and me get **10% of what they lose**. And we lose nothing if they win. Straight cash every Monday. So **(NO RISK)**.
2. They get **40% bonus betting money** for the first deposit they make.
3. They get **20% bonus betting money for every deposit**. After their first deposit, we get 10% of the bonus money each. So this isn't real cash — we get 10% each to gamble with.
4. They get **10% of total losses** excluding bonus betting money (**it does not count**).
5. **Odds are the same as betting websites.**

## Reading the Structure
The arrangement is a standard agent model: the agent is paid from player losses and carries no downside on player wins. Point 4 appears to describe what the *player* gets back — a 10% loss rebate — which is the offer being sold, while point 1 is what the referrer keeps.

Two things worth being precise about before using this with anyone:
- **Points 1 and 4 both say "10% of losses"** but describe different parties. As written it is ambiguous, and ambiguity about who gets paid what is how these arrangements end in arguments.
- **The bonus money exclusion in point 4** is the part players will misunderstand. Losses on bonus money do not count toward the rebate. That needs saying out loud, not burying.

## A Note on What This Is
Taking a cut of other people's gambling losses is regulated activity in New York, and the terms above describe acting as an agent rather than as a player. Worth understanding the legal position before running it, particularly the part about being paid in cash weekly.
