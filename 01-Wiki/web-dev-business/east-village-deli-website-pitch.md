# East Village Deli — Website Revenue Pitch

**Summary:** A conservative revenue projection prepared by Daniel Choi (website development and consulting) for the owner of an established East Village deli in Manhattan. The document makes the case for building a website by quantifying four specific revenue streams: delivery app fee recovery, catering capture, SEO-driven new customers, and credibility-based loss prevention. All figures are deliberately cut in half from industry standard estimates.

## Key Points
- Even the most conservative scenario adds $9,150/year; realistic scenario adds $20,400/year.
- The deli has been open for 10+ years but has no website — a credibility risk with younger customers.
- Direct ordering through a website cuts out DoorDash/Uber Eats/Grubhub commissions (25-30% per order).
- The East Village has untapped catering demand from offices, NYU, production studios, and event venues.
- Contact: Daniel Choi — 917-245-8685 — Manhattan/NYC.

## Revenue Projections (Conservative — All Figures Halved from Industry Standard)

### 1. Delivery App Fee Recovery
Switching customers to direct ordering reduces platform commissions (25-30% per order).
- Monthly: $250–$300
- Annual: $3,000–$3,600

### 2. Catering and Bulk Orders
The neighborhood has offices, NYU, production studios, and event venues not currently reachable without a catering page.
- Monthly: $400–$2,400
- Annual: $4,800–$28,800

### 3. New Customers via Local SEO
A proper website captures high-intent searches like "best deli East Village" that a Google listing alone misses.
- Monthly: $110–$500
- Annual: $1,350–$6,000

### 4. Credibility Loss Prevention
An established business with no website looks closed or unreliable to younger customers who verify online before visiting.
- Annual loss prevention: ~$2,700
