# One — Character Canon

**Summary:** The established facts for One, the main character of a five-person ensemble crime story. He becomes the group's leader without ever seeking it, grew up poor with a sick mother and a younger sister he protects, starts selling drugs at 13 by organizing what older kids were doing badly, and has a natural ability to watch something, work out why it worked, and copy or improve it. His early flaw is that violence has always worked, so he has never had to use the better part of himself.

## Key Points
- **Leadership is never appointed.** He says what he thinks should happen, his plans are better, so people follow.
- His natural ability: **watch, mentally replay, analyze why it worked, then copy or improve it** — applies to fighting, people, and eventually criminal organization.
- **Early flaw:** violence has always worked, so his intelligence goes unused until a situation forces it.
- Enters crime at **13** by seeing older kids selling carelessly and organizing it better.
- **Before 13, he hurts the wrong person** — the first proof that violence creates problems he cannot punch his way out of.
- He never speaks in the opening chapter. That is a deliberate visual rule.

## Core Role
- One is the major main character within the five-person ensemble.
- He becomes the leader naturally — nobody appoints him. He simply says what he thinks should happen or what he wants to do.
- His plans are usually better than everyone else's, so people follow him.
- Leadership becomes necessary because the other kids do not function or move efficiently without him directing things.
- He treats leadership like a full-time responsibility and sees the group as family.
- He becomes somewhat controlling, but not excessively.

## Family
- Grew up poor.
- **No information about his father.**
- His mother has been sick for as long as he can remember and is unable to work. He knows she is sick, picks up her medicine and runs errands for her. She is loving.
- One younger sister. They are very close.
- As he gets involved in crime he distances himself from her somewhat, to keep her away from that world — but stays present whenever she needs him.

## Personality
Quiet · Intimidating · Charismatic without trying · Careful and strategic with words · Good at reading weaknesses · Good at seeing dynamics, causes and perspectives other people miss · Does not chase leadership or attention · Fights when he believes it is needed.

## Natural Ability
He can watch something happen, mentally reimagine it, analyze why and how it worked, and copy or improve it. He sees multiple perspectives at once. This applies to fighting, people, situations, and eventually business and criminal organization.

At first he does not fully use this ability outside physical conflict.

## Early Flaw
One solves problems with violence because violence has always worked for him. He has not needed to use his intelligence as much as he should. Later, a situation forces him to think rather than fight — and that becomes the point where he realizes his mind is worth more than beating people up.

## Entry Into Crime
1. He sees older kids selling drugs in an unorganized, careless way.
2. He recognizes they are operating inefficiently.
3. He finds a better way to organize and position what they are doing.
4. This lets him take customers from others and start making real money.
5. Later he realizes money can materially change his life and solve real problems.

## Early Major Mistake
Before age 13, One hurts the wrong person. That is the first clear proof that solving everything with violence creates problems he cannot punch his way out of.

## Open Questions to Decide
The canon is deliberately incomplete on four points:

**1. Who is the wrong person he hurts?** Options considered: a kid mistaken for an enemy, an older gang member, someone connected to a gang, an innocent bystander, a friend of one of the five, a relative of someone dangerous, a teacher or coach, or simply someone in the wrong place.

**2. What is the consequence?** Starting a larger conflict, getting the group targeted, bringing danger toward his mother or sister, getting suspended or arrested, losing someone's trust, or realizing the person was not actually the problem.

**3. His speaking style.** Options: very few words but every sentence lands; calm and direct; persuasive without sounding persuasive; quietly threatening; uses logic to make people agree; normal with friends but precise under pressure; rarely talks then says exactly what needs saying.

**4. His first money realization.** What does money first concretely change — medicine for his mother, food and necessities without asking anyone, helping his sister, seeing how differently people treat him, independence from adults, or realizing money creates influence and loyalty?

Of the four, **question 4 is the one that shapes the character most.** If money first buys his mother's medicine, he is a provider who drifts into crime. If it first buys him the way people treat him differently, he is something else entirely.
